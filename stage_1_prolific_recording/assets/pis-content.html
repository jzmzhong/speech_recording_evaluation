What is the purpose of the study?

The purpose of this study is to explore and establish the most effective methods for evaluating how faithfully and accurately accents are reproduced in synthesised speech. With the rapid advancement of speech technology, particularly in areas such as voice cloning and speech synthesis, there is a growing need to assess the quality and authenticity of the accents generated by these systems. Despite the ability of these technologies to mimic individual voices, many synthesised recordings fail to faithfully capture the nuances of regional or foreign accents.

By collecting recordings and survey responses from you, this study aims to determine the best strategies, tools, and surveys for accurately measuring accent fidelity in synthetic voices. Ultimately, the goal is to improve the reliability of synthesised speech for a broader range of applications, ensuring that accent reproduction is as authentic and realistic as possible.

Why have I been asked to take part?

You have been invited to take part in the study because you volunteered to participate in the recording of some scripts and provide annotations or ratings on some speech samples. Additionally, your prior language exposure (based on your area of birth, area of residence, primary/first language, etc) makes you ideal for evaluating certain English accents in speech.

Do I have to take part?

No - participation in this study is entirely up to you. You can withdraw from the study at any time, up until August 31st, 2029, without giving a reason. After this point, personal data will be deleted and anonymised data will be combined such that it is impossible to remove individual information from the analysis. Your rights will not be affected. If you wish to withdraw, contact the PI. We will keep copies of your original consent, and of your withdrawal request.

What will happen if I decide to take part?

You will take part in a two-stage Prolific study:

Voice Recording & Cloning: In the first stage, we will record you reading around 35 sentences. We will also collect some general demographic information (e.g. age, gender, language status, etc.). Using a subset of these recordings, we will then generate a synthetic "cloned" version of your voice for use in the next stage.

Speech Evaluation: In the second stage, you will then be tasked to rate or annotate around 35 speech samples, comprising both cloned voices and real recordings. The second stage will be released to you several days after the first stage.

How will your recordings be used and stored?

Usage: Your recordings and cloned voices will be used during the workshop and may be utilized for future academic research. If you wish to delete your data, please contact the PI.

Privacy: We will not distribute your original recordings to the public. With your consent, your recordings may be shared with other researchers from non-commercial institutions.

Compensation

You will be paid £5 for your participation in the first stage, and £10 for your participation in the second stage, which will take around 20 and 40 minutes, respectively.

Are there any risks associated with taking part?

There are no significant risks associated with participation.

Are there any benefits associated with taking part?

Apart from compensation, you will contribute to the research and development of speech science and technology for your accent, as an indirect benefit from our overall project.

What will happen to the results of this study?

The results of this study may be summarised in published articles, reports and presentations. Quotes or key findings will be anonymized: We will remove any information that could, in our assessment, allow anyone to identify you. With your consent, information can also be used for future research. Your data may be archived for a maximum of 3 years. All potentially identifiable data will be deleted within this timeframe if it has not already been deleted as part of anonymization.

Data protection and confidentiality.

Your data will be processed in accordance with Data Protection Law. All information collected about you will be kept strictly confidential. Your data will be referred to by a unique participant number rather than by name. Your data will only be viewed by the researcher/research team (who are Dr. Korin Richmond, Dr. Catherine Lai, Jinzuomu Zhong, and Alice Ross).

All electronic data will be stored on a password-protected encrypted computer, on the School of Informatics' secure file servers, or on the University's secure encrypted cloud storage services (DataShare, ownCloud, or Sharepoint) and all paper records will be stored in a locked filing cabinet in the PI's office. Your consent information will be kept separately from your responses in order to minimise risk.
